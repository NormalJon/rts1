Medieval RTS · placeholder art written as code

Art Sample Sheet

A first set for High Medieval Western Europe, about 1000 to 1250. Every model here is generated by a script and exported as a standard glTF file, the format Godot, Unity and Unreal all import. Nothing was drawn or sculpted by hand.

Team colour

Each model has one material named TeamColor on its banners, shield faces and tunics. The engine recolours that material per player. Named here for the heraldic tinctures.

Drag a model sideways to turn it, up and down to change the camera height. Arrow keys work when a model has focus.

Interface icons

Single-colour SVG on a 32-unit grid. Each is shown large and at 32 px inside a command button.

What this approach can and cannot do

Works well

  • Buildings, walls, siege engines and props. Straight timber and cut stone suit geometry built from boxes, prisms and cylinders.
  • Trees and rocks, where a little randomness in the script reads as natural variety.
  • Consistency. Every file shares one scale (1 unit is 1 metre), one palette, the same origin and the same team-colour slot.
  • Variants. A second house, a taller tower or a later-period keep is a change of numbers, then a rebuild.
  • Stylised units that move by rotating rigid limbs, which is enough at strategy-game camera distance.
  • Interface icons, heraldry and diagrams as SVG.

Does not work

  • Organic shapes. Faces, horses, cloth folds and anything sculpted come out as blocks. The figures above are toy soldiers, not characters.
  • Skinned animation. Joints here do not bend or stretch; limbs are separate solid pieces. Motion is written as formulas, so it looks stiff close up.
  • Textures. There is no painted or photographed surface detail: no stone coursing, wood grain or thatch. Colour is one flat value per face.
  • Art direction by eye. I check results from screenshots, not by looking at the model while shaping it, so proportions take several rounds.
  • Sound, music and voice. None of that is covered here.

The practical reading: this is good for placeholders that are the right size, shape and period, and for structures it may be good enough to keep. For units, an artist or a well-made open-licence pack will look better, and these files show them exactly what to replace.

Rebuild everything with npm run build in art-pipeline/. The models on this page are the exported .glb files themselves, embedded and read by a standard glTF loader.
